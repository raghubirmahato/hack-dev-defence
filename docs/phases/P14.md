## Phase introduction (verbatim)

Requirement prefix `SQLI`. Goal: deliver the capability at the center of the product: **any SQL injection attempt against any form, login page, URL, header or cookie is detected and blocked, and the attacker's IP is banned, even when the origin application is itself vulnerable.** The phase builds the SQL tokenizer and fingerprint engine, the six hard signature families (H1 to H8 of C4.5) including the six mandated payloads, evasion handling, field-kind context policy, the SQLi ban policy, auth-endpoint behavior, NoSQL/LDAP/XPath/GraphQL injection detectors, write-path hardening for second-order attacks, time-based attack detection, the fixture and benign suites, the sqlmap campaign harness, metrics and UI integration.

This phase implements detection and the ban *decision* (the `BanIntent` and the policy table of C4.10). Ban *storage and enforcement* (Redis, kernel) arrive in P16 and the end-to-end lab proof (AC-38, AC-39) is completed there; here the proof is at the engine, inspect-API and proxy-verdict level plus a sqlmap campaign in `monitor` mode through the walking-skeleton proxy.

Doctrine reminders (C4): hard hits ban on first occurrence outside cross-site subresource context; password-field H2 is the one documented relaxation (soft 4 in Balanced); benign names such as `O'Brien` must pass; the mandated set is the six payloads supplied by the operator plus the variant families.

Gate G14 passes when: the mandated set and all variant fixtures are 100% `would_block` with category `sqli` in every vector of C4.2; held-out detection ≥ 95%; benign corpora produce zero false positives; the sqlmap monitor-mode campaign coverage ≥ 95% of injection-bearing requests (exact figure and unflagged classes reported); mutation score on SQLi modules ≥ 80%; fuzz smoke is clean.
