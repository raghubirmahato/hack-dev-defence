### T-0350 · Anti-scraping and crawl abuse controls
`P17` `[DP][SEC]` · deps: T-0340, T-0307 · size: M · owner: dp
**Objective.** Slow or stop abusive bulk collection of content.
**Steps.**
1. Detect scraping patterns: high sequential coverage of URL space (ID increments, pagination walks), consistent timing, low asset ratios, absence of cookies/JS, deep pagination scanning, API enumeration, high bytes-per-client, repeated downloads of large resources.
2. Implement policy actions: tarpit (bounded delays), throttle (reduced rate), challenge, or block; defaults to challenge for repeated patterns and throttle for known crawlers over their allowance; verified bots get documented crawl-delay-like soft limits via 429 with `Retry-After` rather than blocks.
3. Implement per-path "protected content" declarations (product catalogs, listings, pricing) with stricter thresholds.
4. Implement honeypot links optional (hidden links to trap crawlers; owner adds the markup; detection when followed).
5. Provide `robots.txt` assistance (advice only; HDD does not rewrite origin robots).
6. Implement metrics and dashboards (top scraped paths, sources, bytes).
7. Test with scripted crawlers (polite and aggressive) and human browsing traces.
8. Link ATK-194 to ATK-197.
**Deliverables.**
- detectors, policies, UI hooks, tests
**Acceptance.**
- A1: sequential ID walking across 200 items is challenged within the configured window.
- A2: human browsing of 50 pages is not challenged.
- A3: verified bots over allowance receive 429 + `Retry-After` without bans.
- A4: tarpit is capped.
- A5: protected-path declarations adjust thresholds.
**Tests.**
- Simulation; benign replay; unit: coverage metrics.
**Notes.** Scraping can be reduced but not fully prevented; documented limitation.
