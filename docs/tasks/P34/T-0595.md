### T-0595 · Exploit-signature ingestion, staged rollout and the emergency channel
`P34` `[SEC][DP][OPS]` · deps: T-0594, T-0310, T-0255, T-0425, T-0496, T-0497 · size: XL · owner: sec
**Objective.** Turn request-visible exploit shapes into tested, signed rules quickly and safely.
**Steps.**
1. Implement the converter from community templates and advisory-derived patterns to the HDD rule format (match on method, path, headers, parameters, body shape), rejecting constructs HDD cannot evaluate safely and recording what was dropped.
2. Implement the lint (ReDoS check, cost budget, specificity score) and the automatic false-positive gate against `benign-general`, `benign-sql-prose` and **full-request replay corpora** (method, path, headers, cookies and body, in HAR style) per product, so that rules on paths, headers and cookies are exercised and not only string lists; a minimum corpus size and a one-sided 95% upper confidence bound on the false-positive rate are stated with every result; a rule that matches benign traffic is rejected or narrowed to a product and path scope, which is the defined outcome for false-positive-prone rows. Every converted rule needs a human reviewer's approval before it can leave `candidate`.
3. Implement lab proof where a vulnerable container exists (T-0496): the exploit succeeds directly and is blocked through HDD; results recorded `lab-proven` with the container digest.
4. Implement signing and the staged rollout on the ring ladder of T-0425 (internal canary, 5%, 25%, 100% of opted-in sites, with dwell times) with `monitor` before `block` and a stated promotion criterion (no rule hit-rate spike, ban-rate or 4xx/5xx change on matched requests beyond stated bounds for the dwell time). Auto-rollback triggers on those quantitative signals, and a rollback also bulk-unbans every ban attributed to the rolled-back rule id.
5. Implement the **emergency channel**: a signed single-rule push that shortens the staging calendar (the lint and false-positive gate still apply) for KEV entries with confirmed active exploitation. Controls: two-person approval (`PERM-vkb.emergency`), a signing key held offline or in an HSM, emergency rules start in `monitor` or `B` (never a ban) for the first 60 minutes, expire after 24 hours unless confirmed, are limited in count per day and in path breadth, have a per-rule kill switch, and are recorded with notification to operators; the key-compromise and revocation procedure is documented.
6. Implement offline mode: `hddctl vkb apply <bundle>` for air-gapped installs, verified by signature, with a monotonic serial number and an expiry in every bundle (an older serial is refused), and a signer revocation list.
7. Implement the time-to-rule tracker: candidate within 24 hours and monitor rule within 72 hours for request-visible KEV entries, reported as measured, with misses explained.
8. Add tests with synthetic entries, hostile templates (ReDoS, huge patterns), signature failures and rollback.
**Deliverables.**
- converter, lint and gate, lab proof harness, staged rollout, emergency channel, offline bundles, tracker, tests
**Acceptance.**
- A1: a template using unsupported constructs (back-references, lookaround, unbounded nested repetition) is rejected and logged by the lint, and the engine's linear-time guarantee is tested with a worst-case input.
- A2: a candidate rule that matches 1 item of the replay corpus is rejected or narrowed, and the report states the corpus size and the upper confidence bound.
- A3: an emergency push reaches 95% of a 20-node test fleet within 10 minutes, starts in `monitor`, expires after 24 hours and is logged; a single approver cannot publish it.
- A4: an unsigned, tampered, expired or older-serial bundle, and one from a revoked signer, is rejected by `hddctl vkb apply`.
- A5: a rolled-back rule's bans are removed (bulk-unban test), and the tracker report lists, for the recorded KEV fixture, the time-to-rule per entry and flags misses.
**Tests.**
- Converter and lint tests; lab proofs; rollout and rollback tests; signature tests.
**Notes.** The emergency channel never skips the false-positive gate and never starts with a ban; a fast wrong rule is worse than a slow right one.

## Definition of done for this task's tags (section 0.7)

### `[SEC]`: Security-relevant logic

Abuse cases written as tests; STRIDE delta in `docs/STRIDE.md`; no secret in logs; constant-time comparison where secrets are compared; failure modes fail closed; panic-free on hostile input (proptest or fuzz target).

Hot path never awaits the database; bounded memory and queues; criterion benchmark or histogram; fuzz or proptest; behavior when Redis or Postgres is down specified and tested; metric with drop counter.

### `[OPS]`: Deployment, operations, runbooks

Idempotent; dry-run mode; documented undo; SSH-lockout protection for host-affecting changes; restore or rollback exercised.
