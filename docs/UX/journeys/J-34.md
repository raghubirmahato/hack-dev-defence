# J-34 Incident: attack spike

Personas: [Sara](../personas/sara.md), [Omar](../personas/omar.md), [Maya](../personas/maya.md). Emotional state is a design aid, not a measured value.

Trigger: the alert rule fires because one domain sees a sharp rise in attack-shaped requests.

## What could go wrong

| Step | What goes wrong | Recovery action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 2 | The attack is misread as normal traffic. | The alert rule and the overview name the category and the change from the baseline. |
| 4 | The ban blocks a shared address used by real visitors. | The collateral hint names the shared-address risk and a challenge option is offered instead. |
| 6 | The calm window is declared too early. | The window repeats the event count and the mode change stays reversible. |

## Incident timeline

| Phase | Trigger | Who acts | Screen | De-escalation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Detection | The alert rule fires on an attack spike. | The alert channel tells Sara and Omar. | S-054 | A reviewer checks the traffic before any mode change. |
| Under-attack mode | The owner or the analyst decides the traffic is hostile. | Maya enables under attack with a typed confirmation. | S-093 | The mode expires at the stated time unless it is renewed. |
| Analyst actions | The campaign addresses and ASNs are identified. | Sara bans the campaign or creates an exception for a false positive. | S-067 | Every ban carries a short TTL and an undo path. |
| De-escalation | The calm window passes without a new spike. | Maya turns under attack off and records the outcome. | S-093 | The timeline keeps the mode change, the calm window and the reason. |

## Retrospective report outline

1. Summary of the incident window.
2. Detection path and the time to the first alert.
3. Traffic evidence with redacted examples.
4. Actions taken and who authorised each one.
5. False positives and appeals filed during the window.
6. What the protection covered, with the test IDs.
7. What it did not cover, and why.
8. Configuration changes made during the incident.
9. Follow-up items with owners and dates.
